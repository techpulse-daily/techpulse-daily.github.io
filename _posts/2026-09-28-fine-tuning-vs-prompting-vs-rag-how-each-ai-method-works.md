Re‑educating the chef, giving a detailed order ticket, or handing an open textbook—that’s how fine‑tuning, prompting, and RAG work with AI.

Fine‑tuning is like sending your chef back to culinary school to master a new cuisine; you adjust the model’s weights—a deep‑learning tweak—so it reliably serves that style.

Prompting is handing the chef a precise order ticket; you write a clear instruction that the model follows using its existing knowledge, no internal changes needed.

RAG (retrieval‑augmented generation) is like giving the chef an open textbook at service; the model pulls relevant facts from an external source in real time, then weaves them into the answer.

Each method solves a different problem: fine‑tuning for consistent, specialized output; prompting for quick, flexible queries; RAG for up‑to‑date or rare information without retraining.

Now you can see why choosing the right approach matters for speed, cost, and accuracy in your AI projects.

When have you chosen a quick prompt over retraining a model, and what trade‑off did you notice?

## Chef Analogy: Fine‑tune, Prompt, RAG

Three ways to guide an AI, like training or directing a cook.

## Fine‑tuning = Re‑educating the chef

Adjust the model’s internal weights so it learns a new specialty.

## Prompting = Detailed order ticket

Give a clear instruction; the model uses its existing knowledge.

## RAG = Open textbook at service

Model retrieves fresh facts from external data, then generates answer.

## Why pick one?

Each balances speed, cost, and up‑to‑date accuracy differently.
