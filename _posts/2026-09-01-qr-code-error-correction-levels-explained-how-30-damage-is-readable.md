You can tear off 30% of a QR code sticker and your phone still reads it instantly. That magic comes from error correction – a built‑in safety net that adds extra data blocks so the scanner can rebuild missing pieces. QR codes are divided into tiny squares called modules, and some of those modules store error‑correction code – a special pattern that describes how to recover lost information. There are four levels (L, M, Q, H) that trade off how much extra data you store for how much damage you can survive; the higher the level, the more of the code can be missing and still work. When your camera scans, it reads the visible modules, uses the error‑correction pattern to guess the hidden ones, and then decodes the original URL or text. This is why you can keep a QR sticker on a laptop, peel part of it off, and still open the link without a hiccup. You now understand why a QR code can survive damage and still be read instantly.
