Your password is like a house key, and the code sent to your phone is like the secret knock you give to a trusted neighbor before they let you in. Together they form two-factor authentication (2FA) — a security method that requires two different proofs of identity: something you know (the password) and something you have (the phone). When you log in, you first type your password, then you receive a one‑time code on your phone that you enter to confirm it’s really you. The code is a temporary passcode, generated each time you try to access the account, so even if someone steals your password they still can’t get in without that phone. This extra step blocks most automated attacks because bots can’t reach your phone. If you lose your phone, you can use backup codes — pre‑generated one‑time passwords you store safely — to regain access. Now you understand how combining a secret you remember with a device you carry makes your accounts much harder to breach.

## Password + Phone = Two‑Factor Auth

Your password is a key; the phone code is a secret knock.

## How It Works

Enter password, then type a one‑time code sent to your phone.

## Why Bots Fail

Automated attacks can’t read the code on your physical device.

## When Phone Is Lost

Use backup codes—saved one‑time passwords—to recover access.

## What It Protects

Adds a second barrier, keeping accounts safe even if passwords leak.
