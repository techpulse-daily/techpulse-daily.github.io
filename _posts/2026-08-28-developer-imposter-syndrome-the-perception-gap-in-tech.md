Everyone is constantly Googling — senior engineers just know how to phrase the search query. In tech, Imposter syndrome is that feeling you’re faking competence, like you’re using the wrong search terms and still getting answers. It’s common because we all compare our internal checklist of skills to the polished results others share. When you see a senior dev ship a feature, you assume they didn’t need to Google at all, but the truth is they often spent the same minutes typing, just with more precise queries. The brain’s fear response amplifies this gap, turning normal learning into self‑doubt. Recognize that every “aha” moment comes after a search, a trial, or a mistake. By reframing your own Googling as a tool, not a flaw, you reduce the pressure to appear flawless and focus on actual progress. You now understand that Imposter syndrome is a perception gap, not a lack of ability, and that everyone’s success is built on the same habit of asking the right questions.
