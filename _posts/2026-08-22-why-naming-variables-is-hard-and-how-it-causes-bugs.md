A misplaced label on a mystery jar that confuses every cook in the kitchen 6 months later. Just like that jar, a poorly named variable hides its purpose, making future code readers guess its meaning. When you name a variable, you give a mental shortcut—a readable tag—that tells anyone reading the code what data it holds. If the name is vague, like "temp" for a temperature that later becomes a discount rate, the code becomes a puzzle. Clear names act like proper labels: they reduce mental load, prevent bugs caused by misinterpretation, and speed up debugging. Think of scope—where a variable lives in the program—as the jar’s location on the shelf; if the label doesn’t match the shelf, you’ll waste time searching. Consistency is key; if every jar follows the same labeling style, you know exactly where to find sugar or salt. The hardest part is anticipating future readers, not just yourself, and choosing words that stay accurate as the code evolves. Now you understand why a single line of naming can save hours of debugging later.
