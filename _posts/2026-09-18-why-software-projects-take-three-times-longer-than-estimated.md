The last 10% of a feature always eats the other 90% of the timeline.
When you finish the core logic of a feature, you think the job’s done, but the polishing phase—bug fixing, edge‑case handling, UI tweaks—often expands to three times the original estimate. Estimation is the practice of predicting how long work will take; it relies on past data and assumptions about unknowns. Those unknowns are hidden complexities that only surface when the feature is near complete, like discovering a hidden leak after the roof is installed. Because the team’s confidence drops as uncertainty rises, they add safety buffers, but buffers rarely cover the exponential growth of last‑minute work. The result is a three‑fold increase from the original estimate to the actual delivery date. Understanding this pattern lets you set more realistic timelines, allocate extra time for the final polish, and communicate honestly with stakeholders about why software often takes longer than planned. Now you see why the final stretch of a project can dominate the schedule.
